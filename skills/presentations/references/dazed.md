Dazed treats the page as a place to break rules with taste. The type system is often two voices arguing: a display cut that shouts and a neutral body that refuses to. Red — or sometimes an acid green — interrupts the black-and-white like a handwritten note on a proof.

The layouts have nerve. A massive headline will sit next to a tight crop. A full bleed will cut at the shoulder. Copy frequently runs long, and is set like it expects to be read. Nothing centered, nothing safe.

A treatment leaning on Dazed should commit to asymmetry, pick one accent with intention, and leave the rest in strict black and white. Use the accent to mark one or two structural moments, never to decorate.
