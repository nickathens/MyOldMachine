A24 reads as a print house that happens to release films. The wordmark is its own calm grid: a softened A, a deco-adjacent 2 and 4 in Neue Haas Grotesk, held steady on a near-black field. The identity is owed to GrandArmy, who built a system that plays with mid-century deco structure without romanticising it.

The colour discipline is strict. Photography does the screaming; type never does. When accent colour appears — the recurring A24 red, or a warm brass — it is placed once, at the decisive moment, and withdrawn. One-sheets frequently rest on a single editorial image surrounded by type set at magazine scale.

Serifs are editorial, not wedding. GT Sectra and Tiempos Headline show up in campaign materials, paired with the grotesk body. The whole effect is a publisher's confidence. Treatments that borrow from A24 should leave more room than they think they need, commit to one accent colour, and let serif italics carry the emotion.
