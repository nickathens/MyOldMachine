The Ghibli Museum's materials live in the tactile register. Paper bears a tooth, inks sit on the surface. Palette leans to forest greens, warm creams, and a terracotta-to-clay orange that reads like a sunset through rice paper.

Type pairs a humanist serif with a quiet working sans. Illustration is honoured, never ironically. Hand-lettered titles and botanical marks show up next to formal type without friction. The effect is a museum that feels like a garden.

A treatment leaning on Ghibli should avoid sharp geometric sans, refuse pure black, and treat negative space as sky, not margin. Let one hand-drawn element touch the page, however small.
