# Short Film Screenwriting Guide

Specific guidance for scripts under 15 minutes.

## The Economics of a Short Film Script

**1 page = 1 minute.** A 10-minute short is 10 pages. That's not a lot of room. Every word has to earn its place, and every scene has to do at least two jobs.

Academy definition: under 40 minutes. Festival reality: under 15 minutes wins. Under 10 is the sweet spot for selection. Under 5 is extremely competitive but gets programmed more often.

## What Works

### Single Concept
The best short films are built around one idea executed with precision. Not three subplots and a twist. One question, explored fully.

Examples of strong single concepts:
- What happens when you can't lie for one day? (but in 10 minutes, so one conversation)
- A job interview where the applicant slowly realizes the company is a cult
- Two strangers stuck in an elevator who discover they're both running from the same thing

### Limited Scope
- **1-3 locations.** Every new location costs you screen time for establishing shots and transitions
- **2-4 characters.** Every new character costs you screen time for introduction
- **One timeline.** Flashbacks and time jumps eat minutes you don't have
- **One central conflict.** B-plots are a luxury for features

### Compression Techniques
- **Start in media res.** The inciting incident already happened. We open on the aftermath or the middle
- **Let the setting do exposition.** A messy room with empty bottles tells us everything about the character without a word
- **Use objects.** A ring on a table. A packed suitcase. A broken window. Objects communicate backstory without dialogue
- **End on the question, not the answer.** Short films can afford ambiguity. The audience will fill in the meaning themselves. That's often more powerful than a neat resolution

## Structure for Shorts

### The 5-Page Short (5 minutes)
```
Page 1: Setup + Inciting Incident (combine them)
Pages 2-3: Rising tension / single escalating sequence
Page 4: Climax / reversal
Page 5: Resolution or open ending
```

### The 10-Page Short (10 minutes)
```
Pages 1-2: Setup, character, world, the problem
Page 3: The attempt or commitment
Pages 4-7: Complications, escalation, the real conflict reveals itself
Pages 8-9: Climax
Page 10: Resolution / aftermath
```

### The 15-Page Short (15 minutes)
```
Pages 1-3: Setup and inciting incident
Pages 4-5: Debate / plan
Pages 6-11: Rising action with 2-3 complications
Pages 12-13: Climax
Pages 14-15: Resolution and final image
```

## Common Short Film Mistakes

1. **Feature in disguise.** If you need 20 minutes of setup, you're writing a feature. Rethink the scope
2. **Starting too early.** Don't build the world for 3 pages before something happens. The audience gives you 30 seconds before they decide if they're in
3. **Too many characters.** Every character you introduce is a character you need to develop and resolve. In 10 minutes, you can only do this well for 2-3 people
4. **Exposition dumps.** If you need to explain the rules of your world for 2 pages, the concept is too complex for a short
5. **Neat endings.** Life doesn't wrap up neatly, and neither do the best shorts. An unresolved final image that lingers is often stronger than a clean resolution
6. **No visual thinking.** A short film is not a short story read aloud. Think in images, sounds, gestures, spaces. What can the camera see that dialogue can't say?
7. **Over-producing on the page.** You don't need elaborate VFX sequences in a short. The constraint is the strength. One room, two people, the right words: that can be cinema

## What Festival Programmers Look For

- Originality of concept (not just execution)
- Emotional specificity (not generic sadness or generic joy)
- Confidence in pacing (the filmmaker knows when to linger and when to cut)
- A final image that recontextualizes everything before it
- Sound design and music that serve the story, not wallpaper it
- Evidence that the filmmaker has something to say, not just something to show
