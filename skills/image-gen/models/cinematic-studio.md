# Cinematic Studio (Higgsfield)

Covers: cinematic (Cinematic Studio 2.5)

## Prompt Style

Film-oriented direction. This model is tuned for cinematic aesthetics. Think in terms of scenes, not photographs. Describe shots the way a director or DP would.

## Optimal Length

40-80 words. Focus on mood, framing, and atmosphere.

## Key Techniques

- **Scene direction.** Frame prompts as shot descriptions: "Wide shot of a lone figure walking through ankle-deep fog on an empty highway at dusk. Camera low, slightly tilted."
- **Cinematic vocabulary.** Use film terminology: "rack focus from foreground to subject", "motivated lighting from a streetlamp", "anamorphic lens flare", "shallow focus close-up".
- **Color grading language.** Describe the grade: "teal and orange color grade", "desaturated with crushed blacks", "warm amber tones, lifted shadows". The model responds strongly to color direction.
- **Atmosphere over detail.** Prioritize mood-setting elements: weather, time of day, light quality, environmental texture. "Rain-slicked asphalt reflecting neon" communicates more than listing objects.
- **Genre anchoring.** Naming a visual genre helps: "film noir lighting", "Wes Anderson symmetry", "Ridley Scott industrial". One reference per prompt.

## What NOT to Do

- Don't use still-photography keywords (ISO, shutter speed) as the model is film-oriented
- Don't over-specify small props or background details at the expense of mood
- Don't mix conflicting visual genres in one prompt

## Example Refinement

Bad: "cinematic scene, beautiful, dramatic, 4k"
Good: "A silhouetted figure stands at the edge of a rooftop, city skyline stretching behind them. Blue hour, last trace of daylight on the horizon. Sodium vapor streetlights below casting orange pools. Anamorphic bokeh, wide aspect ratio. Moody, restrained color palette, slightly underexposed."
